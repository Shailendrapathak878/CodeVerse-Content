Advanced Plotting • Topic 16

Multiple Data Series & Twin Axes

Compare multiple metrics on a single canvas, master multi-channel visual encoding (color, linestyle, markers), conquer scale mismatch with ax.twinx() and ax.twiny(), build unified legends, and avoid false visual correlations.

📊 Section A — Introduction to Multiple Data Series

Data science mein ek isolated line plot dekhna useful hota hai, lekin asli decision-making tab hoti hai jab aap comparisons karte hain! "Product A ki sales badh rahi hai" — ye achhi baat hai, lekin jab tak aap ye nahi dekhte ki "Product B usse tezi se overtake kar raha hai" ya "Actual sales monthly quota target se 15% peeche hai", tab tak poori picture clear nahi hoti.

Ek Data Series ek specific measurement ya category ka sequence hota hai (jaise Product Alpha ki monthly revenue). Jab hum ek hi Axes par multiple series draw karte hain, toh reader ko instant visual context milta hai: trends, divergences, aur crossover points (intersections).

Figure 16.1: Single Series (Left: Isolated growth curve) vs. Multiple Comparative Series (Right: Flagship vs. Legacy vs. Target, highlighting the April crossover event).
Analogy (दौड़ का मुकाबला): Agar ek athlete akele track par daud raha hai, toh stopwatch time batati hai, lekin ye pata nahi chalta ki wo race jeet raha hai ya haar raha hai. Jab 3 athletes ek sath daudte hain, toh reader ko instant pata chal jata hai ki kaun aage hai, kaun pichhad raha hai, aur kis mod par lead badal rahi hai!

Code Example 1: Comparing Two Competing Products

Dekhiye kaise do simple ax.plot() calls se do series aapas mein compare hoti hain:

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

months = ['Jan', 'Feb', 'Mar', 'Apr', 'May', 'Jun']
prod_a = [35, 42, 48, 55, 68, 80]
prod_b = [50, 48, 45, 52, 60, 65]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))

# Plotting series 1: Product Alpha
ax.plot(months, prod_a, marker='o', color='#0284c7', lw=2.5, label='Product Alpha (SaaS)')

# Plotting series 2: Product Beta
ax.plot(months, prod_b, marker='s', color='#059669', lw=2.2, label='Product Beta (Legacy)')

ax.set_title("Comparative Monthly Revenue Trajectory", fontweight='bold')
ax.set_xlabel("Financial Month")
ax.set_ylabel("Revenue (₹ Lakhs)")
ax.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.4)
ax.legend(loc='upper left')

plt.show()

📈 Section B — Plotting Multiple Lines on the Same Chart

Multiple lines plot karne ke liye aapko alag-alag figures banane ki zaroorat nahi hai. Aap usi ax object par baar-baar ax.plot(x, y, label=...) call kar sakte hain.

Figure 16.2: Quarterly hardware shipments across Model Pro, Model Air, and Model Mini on a unified canvas.

Key Rules of Plotting Multiple Lines

  • Always provide a label: Har plot() call mein label='...' dena mandatory hai, varna ax.legend() blank rahega!
  • Z-Order & Layering: Jo line baad mein plot hoti hai, wo pehle wali ke oopar render hoti hai. Critical highlight line ko hamesha aakhri mein call karein ya zorder=4 set karein.
  • Meaningful variable names: y1, y2 ki jagah hamesha domain-specific names use karein jaise rev_pro, rev_air.

Code Example 3: Hardware Shipments Multi-Line Script

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

quarters = ['Q1-2025', 'Q2-2025', 'Q3-2025', 'Q4-2025', 'Q1-2026', 'Q2-2026']
device_pro = [120, 145, 170, 210, 260, 310]
device_air = [180, 195, 205, 220, 240, 255]
device_mini = [90, 85, 95, 110, 105, 120]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))

# 3 sequential plot calls on the same axes

ax.set_title("Quarterly Hardware Shipments by Device Category", fontweight='bold')
ax.set_ylabel("Units Shipped (Thousands)")

🎨 Section C — Styling Multiple Series for Accessibility

Ek common amateur mistake: 3 ya 4 lines draw kiye aur sabko solid lines bana diya, sirf color alag-alag! Sochiye agar koi student photocopy ya black-and-white print nikalta hai, ya reader ko color-blindness (jaise red-green deficiency) hai — toh saari lines ek jaisi gray dikhengi!

Figure 16.3: Inaccessible styling (Left: only color changes, fails in grayscale) vs. Multi-channel accessible styling (Right: Distinct color + distinct linestyle + distinct marker shapes).
The Triple-Encoding Principle:
Professional data visualization mein har series ko kam se kam 3 distinct visual channels se distinguish karein:
1. Color: High contrast color palettes (e.g. Deep Blue, Emerald Green, Amber).
2. Line Style: Solid ('-'), Dashed ('--'), Dotted (':'), Dash-dot ('-.').
3. Markers: Circles ('o'), Squares ('s'), Triangles ('^'), Diamonds ('d').

Code Example 4: Implementing Triple-Encoding for Accessibility

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

tests = ['Test 1', 'Test 2', 'Test 3', 'Test 4', 'Test 5']
batch_a = [65, 72, 78, 85, 92]
batch_b = [70, 68, 74, 73, 80]
batch_c = [58, 62, 60, 68, 72]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.8))

# Series 1: Solid + Circle + Blue
ax.plot(tests, batch_a, color='#0284c7', linestyle='-', marker='o', markersize=7, lw=2.2, label='Batch A (Solid + Circle)')

# Series 2: Dashed + Square + Green
ax.plot(tests, batch_b, color='#059669', linestyle='--', marker='s', markersize=7, lw=2.2, label='Batch B (Dashed + Square)')

# Series 3: Dotted + Triangle + Amber
ax.plot(tests, batch_c, color='#d97706', linestyle=':', marker='^', markersize=8, lw=2.4, label='Batch C (Dotted + Triangle)')

ax.set_title("Student Examination Performance across Cohorts", fontweight='bold')
ax.set_ylabel("Average Score (%)")
ax.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)
ax.legend(loc='upper left', frameon=True)

plt.show()

🏷️ Section D — Legends for Multiple Series

Multiple series wale chart mein legend hi map hota hai jo reader ko batata hai ki kaunsi line kya represent kar rahi hai. Agar 4 ya 5 series hain, toh vertical single-column legend chart ki valuable space kha sakta hai.

Figure 16.4: Multi-series production metrics with a 2-column centered legend (ncol=2) and an explicit legend title.

Power Parameters of ax.legend()

  • ncol=2 ya ncol=3: Legend items ko horizontal columns mein arrange karta hai, jisse vertical space bachti hai.
  • title="Telemetry Category": Legend box ke upar ek crisp heading add karta hai.
  • frameon=True, facecolor='#ffffff', edgecolor='#cbd5e1': Legend box ko clean white card style deta hai.
  • bbox_to_anchor=(x, y): Legend ko plot ke exact bahar anchor karne ke liye use hota hai.

Code Example 5: Clean Multi-Column Legend

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

days = ['Mon', 'Tue', 'Wed', 'Thu', 'Fri', 'Sat']

target = [130, 135, 145, 150, 160, 170]

# 2-column legend centered neatly at the top

🍝 Section E — Plotting More Than Two Series & Visual Hierarchy

Jab chart mein 5, 6 ya 7 lines aa jaati hain, toh usse data visualization community mein "Spaghetti Plot" kehte hain — jaise katori mein ubla hua chowmein! Har line ek doosre ko kaat rahi hoti hai aur reader ko samajh nahi aata ki dekhna kahan hai.

Figure 16.5: Spaghetti Plot (Left: 5 competing bright colors) vs. Strategic Visual Hierarchy (Right: Muted gray background lines, spotlight on the hero breakout product).
The Hero-Baseline Technique:
Agar aapke paas 5 products hain aur Product A ne 275% growth ki hai:
• Baki 4 products ko soft muted gray (color='#94a3b8') aur patli dashed lines (lw=1.2) mein daal dein.
• Hero product (Product A) ko bold vibrant blue (color='#0284c7', lw=3.0) mein marker ke sath spotlight karein!
Ise kehte hain Visual Hierarchy. Reader ki aankh seedha main insight par jaati hai.

Code Example 6: Crafting Strategic Visual Hierarchy

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

months = ['Jan', 'Feb', 'Mar', 'Apr', 'May', 'Jun', 'Jul', 'Aug']
p1 = [20, 24, 28, 35, 42, 50, 62, 75] # Hero product
p2 = [40, 38, 42, 45, 43, 44, 46, 48]
p3 = [30, 32, 31, 33, 30, 32, 31, 30]
p4 = [15, 18, 17, 19, 22, 20, 21, 23]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))

            arrowprops=dict(facecolor='#0284c7', edgecolor='#0369a1', arrowstyle='->', lw=1.5),
            fontweight='bold', fontsize=8.5, color='#0369a1',
            bbox=dict(boxstyle='round,pad=0.3', facecolor='#e0f2fe', edgecolor='#0284c7'))

ax.set_title("Product Portfolio Growth: Spotlight on Breakout Category", fontweight='bold')
ax.set_ylabel("Sales Units")
ax.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)
ax.legend(loc='upper left', frameon=True)

plt.show()

⚠️ Section G — Comparing Data with Different Ranges (The Scale Mismatch Problem)

Sochiye aapko do metrics compare karni hain:
1. Website Visitors: 5,000 se 12,000 visitors per day.
2. Conversion Rate: 2.0% se 4.5%.
Agar aap in dono ko bina soche ek hi Y-axis par plot kar denge, toh Y-axis ka scale 0 se 12,000 tak expand ho jayega. Nateeja? Conversion rate ki line 0 ke floor par ekdum dead flat line ban jayegi! Reader ko lagega conversion zero ho gaya!

Figure 16.7: The Scale Mismatch Trap (Left: Conversion rate squashed to flat line at 0) vs. Relative Normalization (Right: 0-100% velocity tracking).

3 Valid Ways to Handle Scale Mismatch

  1. Separate Subplots with sharex=True: Dono metrics ko upar-neeche do alag panels mein draw karein. Zero ambiguity!
  2. Mathematical Normalization: Dono series ko Min-Max scaler se 0–100% index score mein convert kar lein taki relative trend compare ho sake.
  3. Twin Axes via ax.twinx(): Left side par Visitors ka scale aur Right side par Conversion % ka scale attach karein!

🔀 Section H — Introduction to Twin Axes (twinx vs. Subplots)

Twin Axes ka matlab hota hai: ek hi plotting area (canvas) ke andar do independent coordinate axes ka coexist karna. twinx() ek naya Axes create karta hai jo existing Axes ka X-axis share karta hai, lekin uske paas apna independent Y-axis hota hai right side par.

Figure 16.8: Two distinct design philosophies: Stacked Subplots (Left: complete separation of units) vs. Twin Y-Axis (Right: compact correlation mapping).
The False Intersection Trap:
Twin axes use karte waqt hamesha satark rahein! Do lines jahan ek doosre ko cross karti hain, wo asli intersection nahi hota — wo sirf is baat par depend karta hai ki left aur right Y-axes ke ylim limits kya hain! Kabhi bhi do alag units (jaise ₹ Lakhs aur % Conversion) ke cross point ko 'break-even' mat samjhein!

Subplots vs. TwinX: Selection Matrix

Criteria Separate Stacked Subplots Twin Axes (ax.twinx)
Unit clarity ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Highest (Zero confusion) ⭐⭐⭐ Requires careful color-coding
Compactness Requires taller figure (e.g. 8x8) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Highly compact (single panel)
Direct Correlation Eye travels between panels ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Immediate shape alignment
Mobile Screen fit Easy vertical scrolling Needs clear font sizes to avoid clutter

🛠️ Section I — Using ax.twinx() for a Secondary Y-Axis

Chaliye ax.twinx() ko step-by-step master karte hain:

Figure 16.9: Dual Y-axis implementation: Monthly Revenue (Left Y-axis in blue) vs. Fulfilled Orders (Right Y-axis in emerald green).

The 4-Step Golden Recipe for ax.twinx()

  1. Create Base Axes: fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))
  2. Plot Primary Series on ax1: Left Y-axis label aur tick colors ko line ke color se match karein.
  3. Spawn Secondary Axes: ax2 = ax1.twinx()
  4. Plot Secondary Series on ax2: Right Y-axis label aur ticks ko secondary line ke color se match karein!

Code Example 8: Revenue vs. Order Count with ax.twinx()

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

months = ['Jan', 'Feb', 'Mar', 'Apr', 'May', 'Jun']
rev_lakhs = [32, 45, 40, 58, 72, 85]
orders = [850, 1150, 1020, 1420, 1750, 2100]

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(8.5, 4.8))

# 1. Primary Y-axis (Revenue)
color1 = '#0284c7'
line1 = ax1.plot(months, rev_lakhs, marker='o', color=color1, lw=2.5, label='Revenue (₹ Lakhs)')
ax1.set_xlabel("Financial Month")
ax1.set_ylabel("Revenue (₹ Lakhs)", color=color1, fontweight='bold')
ax1.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=color1)
ax1.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)

# 2. Spawn Secondary Y-axis (Orders)
ax2 = ax1.twinx()
color2 = '#059669'
line2 = ax2.plot(months, orders, marker='s', color=color2, lw=2.2, linestyle='--', label='Orders Fulfilled')
ax2.set_ylabel("Fulfilled Order Count", color=color2, fontweight='bold')
ax2.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=color2)

# 3. Combine legends from both axes!
lines = line1 + line2
labels = [l.get_label() for l in lines]
ax1.legend(lines, labels, loc='upper left')

ax1.set_title("E-Commerce Revenue & Order Volume Synchronization", fontweight='bold')
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

🏷️ Section J — Legends & Annotations with Twin Axes

Jab aap do alag Axes (ax1 aur ax2) use karte hain, toh ek bada catch hota hai: ax1.legend() ko ax2 ke baare mein kuch nahi pata hota! Agar aap seedha ax1.legend() call karenge, toh sirf pehli line legend mein aayegi, doosri gayab ho jayegi!

Figure 16.10: Complete twin-axis architecture featuring a consolidated 2-line legend and color-matched callout annotations for traffic and latency spikes.
The Twin-Axis Legend Solution:
Dono lines ke handles aur labels ko extract karke combine karein:
lines = line1 + line2
labels = [l.get_label() for l in lines]
ax1.legend(lines, labels, loc='upper left')

Code Example 9: Traffic QPS vs. Microservice Latency Spike

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

hours = np.arange(8, 21)
traffic = [120, 180, 310, 450, 520, 480, 510, 620, 750, 680, 540, 380, 220]
latency = [45, 48, 52, 68, 85, 80, 82, 110, 165, 140, 95, 60, 48]

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(9.5, 5))

c1, c2 = '#0284c7', '#dc2626'
l1 = ax1.plot(hours, traffic, marker='o', color=c1, lw=2.2, label='Server QPS')
ax1.set_xlabel("Business Hour (08:00 to 20:00)")
ax1.set_ylabel("Traffic (QPS)", color=c1, fontweight='bold')
ax1.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=c1)
ax1.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)

ax2 = ax1.twinx()
l2 = ax2.plot(hours, latency, marker='^', color=c2, lw=2.2, linestyle='--', label='P99 Latency (ms)')
ax2.set_ylabel("P99 Response Latency (ms)", color=c2, fontweight='bold')
ax2.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=c2)

# Consolidated legend
lines = l1 + l2
labels = [l.get_label() for l in lines]
ax1.legend(lines, labels, loc='upper left', frameon=True)

# Color-coded targeted callouts
ax1.annotate('Peak: 750 QPS', xy=(16, 750), xytext=(11.5, 700),
             arrowprops=dict(facecolor=c1, edgecolor=c1, arrowstyle='->', lw=1.5),
             fontweight='bold', color='#0369a1')

ax2.annotate('Latency Spike: 165ms', xy=(16, 165), xytext=(17.2, 145),
             arrowprops=dict(facecolor=c2, edgecolor=c2, arrowstyle='->', lw=1.5),
             fontweight='bold', color='#b91c1c')

ax1.set_title("Cloud Microservice Telemetry: Traffic vs. Latency", fontweight='bold')
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

🔄 Section K — Using ax.twiny() for a Secondary X-Axis

Jaise twinx() Y-axis ko duplicate karta hai, vaise hi ax.twiny() ek secondary X-axis create karta hai jo top of the chart par render hota hai. Ye tab use hota hai jab X-axis ke do equivalent units ya alternate representations hote hain (jaise Temperature in Celsius & Fahrenheit, ya Elapsed Time in Seconds & Minutes).

Figure 16.11: Speed of sound in dry air mapped across dual horizontal temperature scales (Bottom X: Celsius °C; Top X: Fahrenheit °F).

Code Example 10: Speed of Sound with twiny() Dual Scales

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

temp_c = np.linspace(-10, 40, 60)
speed_sound = 331.3 * np.sqrt(1 + temp_c / 273.15)

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 4.5))

# Bottom X-axis: Celsius
color_c = '#0284c7'
ax1.plot(temp_c, speed_sound, color=color_c, lw=2.5)
ax1.set_xlabel("Chamber Temperature (Celsius °C)", color=color_c, fontweight='bold')
ax1.set_ylabel("Speed of Sound (m/s)")
ax1.tick_params(axis='x', labelcolor=color_c)
ax1.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)

# Top X-axis: Fahrenheit
ax2 = ax1.twiny()
# Synchronize limits: F = C * 9/5 + 32
ax2.set_xlim(ax1.get_xlim()[0] * 9/5 + 32, ax1.get_xlim()[1] * 9/5 + 32)
color_f = '#d97706'
ax2.set_xlabel("Equivalent Temperature (Fahrenheit °F)", color=color_f, fontweight='bold')
ax2.tick_params(axis='x', labelcolor=color_f)

ax1.set_title("Acoustic Physics: Speed of Sound vs. Temperature", fontweight='bold', pad=15)
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

🧭 Section L — Sharing Time Axes and Choosing the Right Comparison

Jab aapke paas multiple time-series datasets hote hain, toh kaunsa visual design pick karein? Ye simple decision flowchart follow karein:

Figure 16.12: The Three Architectural Archetypes: 1. Same units -> Single Axes; 2. Correlated different units -> ax.twinx(); 3. Uncorrelated or distinct metrics -> Stacked Subplots.
The 3 Decision Archetypes:
• Case 1: Same Unit & Range (e.g. 3 Products sales in Lakhs): Plot all on the same Axes with multi-channel line styling.
• Case 2: Shared Time Axis + Strongly Correlated Metrics (e.g. Temperature & Humidity, or Traffic & Latency): Use ax.twinx() with distinct color-coding.
• Case 3: Uncorrelated Metrics or Complex Distributions (e.g. Marks & Attendance & Category ranking): Use separate subplots with sharex=True!

Project 3: IoT Industrial Chamber Thermal & Moisture Telemetry

Scenario: Climate testing chamber ke 24-ghante ke industrial cycle ka real-time telemetry dashboard jisme temperature, humidity, aur safe warning threshold (26°C) ka combined monitoring ho.

Figure 16.15: Project 3 IoT Industrial environmental monitoring dashboard.
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

hours = np.linspace(0, 24, 48)
temp = 22.0 + 5.0 * np.sin(np.pi * hours / 12) + np.random.normal(0, 0.15, len(hours))
humidity = 65.0 - 15.0 * np.sin(np.pi * hours / 12) + np.random.normal(0, 0.4, len(hours))

fig, ax1 = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 5))

c1, c2 = '#dc2626', '#0284c7'
line1 = ax1.plot(hours, temp, color=c1, lw=2.2, label='Chamber Temperature (°C)')
ax1.set_xlabel("Chamber Operating Hour (24-Hour Industrial Cycle)")
ax1.set_ylabel("Temperature (°C)", color=c1, fontweight='bold')
ax1.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=c1)
ax1.grid(True, linestyle='--', alpha=0.35)

ax2 = ax1.twinx()
line2 = ax2.plot(hours, humidity, color=c2, lw=2.2, linestyle='--', label='Relative Humidity (% RH)')
ax2.set_ylabel("Relative Humidity (% RH)", color=c2, fontweight='bold')
ax2.tick_params(axis='y', labelcolor=c2)

# Safety threshold
thresh = ax1.axhline(26.0, color='#b91c1c', linestyle=':', lw=1.5, label='Safety Limit (26°C)')

# Combined legend with custom artists
lines = line1 + line2 + [thresh]
labels = [l.get_label() for l in lines]
ax1.legend(lines, labels, loc='upper left', frameon=True)

ax1.set_title("Project 3: IoT Industrial Chamber Thermal & Moisture Telemetry", fontweight='bold')
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

⚠️ Section N — Common Mistakes & Debugging Guide

Twin axes aur multiple data series mein sabse zyada aane wale bugs aur unka instant fix:

Figure 16.16: Debugging the Classic Twin-Axis Incomplete Legend Trap: ax1.legend() drops series B (Left) vs. Combined line handles (Right).
Trap / Bug Kyun Aata Hai? (Root Cause) Instant Production Fix
Incomplete Legend ax1.legend() sirf ax1 ki lines ko scan karta hai, ax2 ko ignore kar deta hai. Lines ko combine karein: lines = l1 + l2; ax1.legend(lines, [l.get_label() for l in lines]).
Missing Secondary Y-Label Developer ax2.set_ylabel() lagana bhool gaya. Reader units nahi samajh pata. Always label both axes: ax2.set_ylabel("Conversion %", color='green').
Grid Lines Collision Dono axes (ax1 aur ax2) par grid lagane se lines criss-cross ho jati hain. Sirf primary axis par grid on rakhein: ax1.grid(True), ax2.grid(False).
False Crossover Illusion Reader samajhta hai ki do alag units ki lines jahan milti hain wo koi magic break-even point hai. Explain in caption ki intersection arbitrary hai aur axis scaling par depend karta hai.

10 Conceptual Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

  1. Ek hi chart par do alag-alag lines plot karne ke liye kya karna hota hai?
    A) Alag Figure banana padta hai
    B) Usi Axes par do baar ax.plot() call karna hota hai
    C) Matplotlib band karke dobara open karna hota hai
    D) plt.double() call karna hota hai
    Answer: B (Repeated ax.plot calls on the same Axes).
  2. Agar chart grayscale mein print ho raha ho, toh sabse best accessibility strategy kya hai?
    A) Sirf different colors use karna
    B) Multi-channel encoding: Colors + Linestyles + Markers
    C) Lines ko mita dena
    D) DPI 50 kar dena
    Answer: B (Triple-encoding ensures distinction even in black & white).
  3. ax.twinx() kaunsi axis ko share karta hai?
    A) Y-axis
    B) X-axis
    C) Dono axes
    D) Koi bhi nahi
    Answer: B (twinx shares the horizontal X-axis and creates a new Y-axis).
  4. ax.twiny() kahan par render hota hai?
    A) Bottom of the plot
    B) Left of the plot
    C) Top of the plot
    D) Right of the plot
    Answer: C (Top horizontal boundary).
  5. Agar ax1 aur ax2 dono par lines plotted hain, toh ax1.legend() call karne par kya hota hai?
    A) Dono lines ka legend automatic ban jata hai
    B) Python crash ho jata hai
    C) Sirf ax1 ki lines aati hain, ax2 ki drop ho jaati hain
    D) Ek bhi line nahi aati
    Answer: C (ax1 only inspects its own artists; handles must be merged manually).
  6. Jab do series ki values mein bohot bada numerical difference ho (e.g. 10,000 visitors vs 3% conversion), toh kya problem hoti hai?
    A) Graph explode ho jata hai
    B) Chhoti value wali series zero par flat line ban jaati hai
    C) Colors fade ho jate hain
    D) Computer freeze ho jata hai
    Answer: B (Scale mismatch squashes smaller magnitude data).
  7. 5 ya 6 se zyada competing lines wale cluttered chart ko kya kehte hain?
    A) Pizza plot
    B) Spaghetti plot
    C) Burger chart
    D) Noodle diagram
    Answer: B (Spaghetti plot).
  8. Spaghetti plot ko clean karne ka sabse effective tareeqa kya hai?
    A) Sabhi lines delete kar dena
    B) Visual Hierarchy: Mute baseline lines in gray and spotlight the hero line
    C) Font size 4pt kar dena
    D) Sabhi lines ko red kar dena
    Answer: B (Hero-baseline visual hierarchy).
  9. Do alag units wali series ke crossover point ke baare mein kaunsa statement sach hai?

    C) Wo stock market crash batata hai
    D) Wo error hai
    Answer: B (Intersection across twin axes is an artifact of axis scaling).
