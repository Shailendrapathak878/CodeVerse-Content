Why HTML Entities Are Needed

In HTML, certain characters are reserved because the browser parser interprets them as markup syntax. For example, typing a less-than sign (<) makes the browser think a new tag is starting.

To display reserved characters or symbols not available on standard keyboards, HTML provides Character Entities.

02

Entity Syntax and Core Characters

An entity begins with an ampersand (&) and terminates with a semicolon (;):

  • &lt; : Less-than sign (<)
  • &gt; : Greater-than sign (>)
  • &amp; : Ampersand sign (&)
  • &quot; : Double quote (")
  • &apos; : Single quote / apostrophe (')
  • &nbsp; : Non-breaking space (prevents words from breaking onto a new line)
03

Typography and Currency Symbols

Entities are also widely used for professional typography and symbols:

  • &copy; : Copyright symbol (©)
  • &reg; : Registered trademark (®)
  • &trade; : Trademark (™)
  • &mdash; : Em-dash (—)
  • &euro;, &pound;, &yen; : Currency symbols (€, £, ¥)

Common Mistakes & Pro Tips

⚠️ Common Trap: Omitting the closing semicolon

Problem: Writing © or < without the trailing semicolon may render unpredictably or fail completely.

Fix: Always close every entity with a semicolon: © and <.
