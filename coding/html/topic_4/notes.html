Headings, Paragraphs & Line Breaks

Transform chaotic text into an organized, readable, accessible document. Master heading hierarchies from <h1> to <h6>, the semantic block behavior of paragraphs <p>, controlled line breaks with <br>, and thematic shifts with <hr>.

⏱ Estimated Read: 15 mins
📚 Level: Beginner
✅ Prerequisites: Topics 1 – 3
💻 Practice: Semantic Article Architecture

📊 1. The Heading Hierarchy (<h1> through <h6>)

HTML gives us six levels of headings: <h1>, <h2>, <h3>, <h4>, <h5>, and <h6>.

Beginners often mistakenly think of headings as simple text size controls. In modern web standards, headings are semantic structural landmarks. They create an invisible table of contents that screen readers and search engines use to understand your entire webpage!

Figure 4.1: Newspaper Heading Structure — Main Headline (h1) down to Subsections (h2 → h3)
👑 The Golden Rules of HTML Headings
  1. Strictly One <h1> per Page: Your <h1> represents the title of the entire document (like the title on the cover of a book). Having multiple <h1> elements dilutes your SEO ranking and disorients screen reader users.
  2. Never Skip Heading Levels: Always descend sequentially: <h1> → <h2> → <h3>. Never jump directly from <h1> to <h4> just because you want smaller visual text! (Use CSS for sizing instead).
  3. Never Use Headings for Bold Text: If you just want a single word or sentence to appear large or bold inside a paragraph, use CSS or <strong>, NOT an <h3>!

📝 2. Paragraphs (<p>) & Block-Level Flow

The <p> tag defines a paragraph of textual content.

Paragraphs are block-level elements. That means:

  • They always start on a brand-new line on the screen.
  • They automatically stretch across the full available width of their parent container.
  • Browsers automatically apply vertical margin (white space) before and after every paragraph to prevent text from colliding.
🪄 Browser Whitespace Collapsing (A Crucial Quirks Lesson)
If you type multiple spaces or hit Enter ten times inside your HTML code: 
<p>
  Hello                 World!
  
  How are you?
</p>
The browser will collapse all consecutive spaces and line breaks into a single single space: "Hello World! How are you?".
To create intentional line breaks or thematic shifts, you must use <br> or <hr>.

✂️ 3. Line Breaks (<br>) and Thematic Breaks (<hr>)

Element Type Correct Semantic Purpose Common Misuse (Anti-Pattern)
<br> Void Tag (Inline) Forces a line break within a single line of flow where the line division is part of the actual content (e.g., postal mailing addresses or poems). Anti-pattern: Writing <br><br><br> to create visual spacing between two paragraphs! (Spacing must always be controlled via CSS margin).
<hr> Void Tag (Block) Semantically represents a thematic break or major transition between distinct scenes, topics, or sections of a document. Anti-pattern: Using <hr> purely for visual decoration or border lines. (Use CSS border-bottom instead).

⚠️ 5. Common Beginner Traps

❌ Skipping Heading Levels
<h1>Main Site Title</h1>
<h4>Subheading Here</h4>

Trap: Skipping <h2> and <h3> breaks assistive outline tools for screen readers.

✅ Sequential Progression
<h1>Main Site Title</h1>
<h2>Subheading Here</h2>

Fix: Always step down one heading tier at a time.

❌ Stacking <br> for Spacing
<p>First paragraph.</p>
<br><br><br>
<p>Second paragraph.</p>

Trap: Stacking line breaks causes uncontrollable layout issues across different device screens.

✅ Natural Paragraph Margin
<p>First paragraph.</p>
<p>Second paragraph.</p>

Fix: Rely on native paragraph margins, or adjust margin via CSS.
