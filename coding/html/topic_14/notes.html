01

Why Semantic HTML Matters

Semantic HTML refers to using tags that convey the meaning of content rather than just its appearance. Writing clean semantic markup brings three major advantages:

  • Accessibility (a11y): Assistive technologies build accessible landmark navigation trees so blind users can jump directly to navigation, main content, or footers.
  • SEO: Search engine spiders understand the primary content vs sidebars and boilerplate footers.
  • Maintainability: Developers can instantly understand code structure without deciphering nested 'div-soup'.
02

Key HTML5 Landmark Elements

Modern web layouts are structured around landmarks:

  • <header>: Introductory content, branding logos, and top navigation.
  • <nav>: Primary navigation links.
  • <main>: The central, unique content of the page (only one per document).
  • <article>: Independent, self-contained content that makes sense on its own (blog post, news item, forum thread).
  • <section>: Thematic grouping of content, typically with its own heading.
  • <aside>: Content tangentially related to the main topic (sidebars, author bio, related links).
  • <footer>: Footer information, copyrights, contact links, and legal disclosures.
