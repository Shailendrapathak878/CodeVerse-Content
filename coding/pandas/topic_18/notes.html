1. Introduction — What are DateTime and Time Series Data?

Real-world businesses aur modern physical devices continuously generate data with timestamps. Har e-commerce order, college attendance check-in, ATM swipe, stock trade, aur smart factory sensor reading ke sath ek exact point in time attach hota hai. Yeh chronological stream Time Series Data kehlata hai.

💡 Real-Life Scenarios: Why Temporal Precision Matters

Scenario A — College RFID Attendance:
University ke main gate pe student ID swipe hota hai: "2026-10-15 08:55:23". College administration ko yeh dekhna hai: Kitne students on-time aaye? Kis din (Monday vs Friday) attendance drop hoti hai? Average campus stay duration kitna hai?

Scenario B — E-Commerce Flash Sale Telemetry:
Ek online store pe festive season mein 10,000 orders aate hain. Product leads ko analyze karna hai: Kaunse ghante (e.g. 8 PM - 10 PM) sales peak karti hai? Daily revenue ki 7-day rolling velocity kya hai? Delivery dispatch duration kitni hai?

Scenario C — IoT Cold-Storage Telemetry:
Pharmaceutical vaccine storage mein har 5 minute mein sensor temperature log karta hai: "2026-10-15 10:05:00 → 4.2°C". Agar 1 hour ka rolling average threshold cross kare ya reading drop ho jaye, immediate emergency alarm trigger hona chahiye!

Figure 18.1: The continuum of temporal structures — Date, Time, Timestamp, and Time Interval along a physical timeline.

Core Concepts Introduced in This Topic:

  • Date: Ek calendar day without time (e.g., 2026-10-15).
  • Time: Wall-clock time without calendar date (e.g., 09:30:00).
  • Timestamp (Datetime): Ek specific point in physical time combining date and clock time (e.g., 2026-10-15 09:30:00).
  • Time Interval (Duration): Do timestamps ke beech ka elapsed time (e.g., Timedelta('8 hours 15 minutes')).
  • Chronological Order: Data points arranged strictly by time progression from earliest to latest.
Figure 18.2: Date strings fail at alphabetical sorting vs real datetime64[ns] timestamps sorting in mathematical order.
⚠ Why Dates Are Imported as Strings by Default

Jab aap CSV ya Excel file load karte hain (pd.read_csv()), Pandas by default dates ko raw text (object dtype) manta hai. Agar aap text dates pe sort_values() karenge, toh "15-10-2026" character '1' hone ki wajah se "02-01-2027" se pehle sort ho jayega! Real datetime64[ns] conversion ke baad hi true chronological sorting aur mathematical time intelligence unlock hoti hai.

3. Converting Strings using pd.to_datetime()

Pandas ka primary weapon dates parse karne ke liye pd.to_datetime() hai. Yeh function highly intelligent hai, lekin production data pipelines mein teen parameters master karna zaroori hai:

Figure 18.3: The pd.to_datetime() ingestion workflow — format directives, ambiguous resolution, and errors='coerce' quarantine.

The Three Core Arguments of pd.to_datetime():

  • format=: Explicitly specifies the date format (e.g. "%d-%m-%Y"). Yeh parsing speed ko 5x to 10x fast kar deta hai aur unexpected mismatches prevent karta hai!
  • dayfirst=True: Ambiguous dates jaise "03/04/2026" ko resolve karta hai. dayfirst=True means 3rd April (Indian/European standard); dayfirst=False means March 4th (US standard).
  • errors="coerce": Agar dataset mein corrupted text (e.g. "InvalidDate", "N/A") ho, toh program crash hone ke bajaye unhe gracefully NaT (Not a Time) mein convert kar deta hai.
🚨 Trap: Silently Hiding Bad Data with errors="coerce"

errors="coerce" use karne ke baad hamesha df['col'].isna().sum() check karein!
Agar aap check nahi karenge, toh 100 corrupted dates silently NaT ban jayengi aur downstream revenue ya attendance calculation se drop ho jayengi without any warning!

Filtering Boundaries: Inclusive vs. Exclusive

Jab aap date filter karte hain (jaise df['date'] <= '2026-10-07'), agar column mein time component bhi present hai (jaise 2026-10-07 19:15:00), toh string '2026-10-07' internally 2026-10-07 00:00:00 (midnight) evaluate hota hai!

9. Date Arithmetic and Offsets — Timedelta vs. DateOffset

Aksar developers sochte hain ki kisi date mein 1 mahina add karne ke liye + pd.Timedelta(days=30) kar dena kaafi hai. Yeh ek bohot bada financial bug create karta hai!

Figure 18.9: Fixed absolute duration Timedelta(days=30) vs Calendar semantic month shift DateOffset(months=1).
⚠ The Critical Difference: Clock Duration vs. Calendar Semantics

pd.Timedelta(days=30): Strictly adds 30 × 24 = 720 hours. If you start on January 31st, adding 30 days lands on March 2nd (because February has only 28 days)!
pd.DateOffset(months=1): Understands human Gregorian calendar semantics! Adding 1 month to January 31st snaps cleanly to February 28th (the end of the next calendar month).

10. Resampling with resample() — Downsampling and Upsampling

Jab aapke paas high-frequency data ho (jaise har second ya minute ke e-commerce orders ya sensor pings) aur aapko higher-level trends dekhne hon (jaise Daily ya Monthly sales), tab regular groupby() ke bajaye Pandas ka powerful resample() method use hota hai.

Resampling time series data ke frequency rate ko change karne ka process hai. Resampling do directions me kaam karti hai:

  • Downsampling (Higher to Lower Frequency): Minutes ya Hours ko Days ya Months me condense karna. Isme multiple data points aggregate hokar ek single summary number banate hain (e.g., sum(), mean(), max()).
  • Upsampling (Lower to Higher Frequency): Daily ya Monthly data ko Hourly ya Daily grid par expand karna. Isme new empty slots create hote hain jinhe fill (jaise ffill()) ya interpolate karna padta hai.
⚠ Critical Prerequisite for resample()

resample() call karne se pehle DataFrame ya Series ka Index ek valid DatetimeIndex hona zaroori hai. Agar datetime kisi regular column me hai, pehle df.set_index('timestamp') call karein ya on='timestamp' parameter pass karein.

11. asfreq() vs. resample() — Point Sampling vs. Bin Aggregation

Bohot se beginners asfreq() aur resample() ko confuse karte hain kyunki dono kisi time series ko regular frequency grid par standardize karte hain. Lekin unka internal mechanism bilkul alag hota hai:

Figure 18.12 — Architecture comparison: asfreq() samples exact timestamp values, whereas resample() collects all records in an interval bin and aggregates them.
Feature resample('10min') asfreq('10min')
Core Purpose Bin-based mathematical aggregation Instantaneous point snapshot / re-indexing
Handling Observations Collects all points falling inside [10:00, 10:10) and applies sum(), mean(), etc. Sirf exactly 10:00:00, 10:10:00, 10:20:00 ke timestamps ko lookup karta hai. Beech ke points ignore ho jaate hain.

14. Time Zones & UTC Conversion — Naive vs. Aware Timestamps

Global systems jaise Amazon, Netflix, ya multinational banking servers alag-alag countries se transactions receive karte hain. Agar ek transaction London me raat ke 11:30 PM par hua aur doosra Mumbai me subah ke 05:00 AM par, to chronological order decide karne ke liye universal clock reference zaroori hota hai.

Pandas me datetime objects do tarah ke hote hain:

  • Timezone-Naive (Default): Isme koi geographic location ya UTC offset info nahi hoti (e.g., 2026-10-15 12:00:00). System ko pata nahi hota ki yeh IST hai, GMT hai, ya EST.
  • Timezone-Aware: Isme standard IANA timezone database ka tag hota hai (e.g., 2026-10-15 12:00:00+00:00 for UTC ya +05:30 for Asia/Kolkata).
Figure 18.16 — Architecture diagram showing the progression from Naive timestamp to UTC localization via tz_localize() and regional conversion via tz_convert().
Operation Pandas Method Initial State Resulting State
Localize s.dt.tz_localize('UTC') Naive (No timezone attached) Aware (Tag attached, clock digits unchanged)
Convert s.dt.tz_convert('Asia/Kolkata') Aware (Must already have a timezone) Aware (Clock digits shifted to new zone offset)
❌ The Classic Trap: Direct Comparison of Naive & Aware Timestamps

Pandas will raise a strict TypeError: Cannot compare tz-naive and tz-aware timestamps agar aap naive aur aware objects ko compare ya subtract karenge. Golden Rule: Always standardize your entire data pipeline to UTC first!

15. Missing & Irregular Time Series Data — Detection, Reindexing & Interpolation

Real-world time series data kabhi perfectly continuous nahi hota. Wi-Fi drops, battery exhaust, sensor disconnects, ya weekend market holidays ke wajah se series me gaps aa jate hain.

17. Decision Tree & 14 Common DateTime Traps

Time series data handling me sabse zyada silent bugs aur runtime crashes aate hain. Niche diye gaye decision tree flowchart aur comprehensive troubleshooting guide ko follow karein taaki aapka production code 100% robust aur bug-free rahe.

Figure 18.19 — Decision Architecture: How to choose between resample(), rolling(), shift(), asfreq(), and groupby() based on your analytical goal.
# Common Trap / Symptom Underlying Root Cause Production Fix & Best Practice
1 ParserError: Unknown string format Dataset contains corrupted text strings or mixed dirty formats. Use pd.to_datetime(s, errors='coerce') to convert corrupt rows to NaT without crashing.
2 Silent date swap (Month & Day swapped) Parsing Indian/European DD/MM/YYYY strings with American MM/DD/YYYY default. Explicitly provide dayfirst=True or pass strict format='%d/%m/%Y'.
3 AttributeError: 'Series' object has no attribute 'year' Calling datetime attributes directly on a Series without the .dt namespace accessor. Always write df['col'].dt.year instead of df['col'].year.
4 AttributeError: 'DatetimeIndex' object has no attribute 'dt' Trying to use .dt on index or calling index properties on non-datetime index. For index, access attributes directly: df.index.year or df.index.day_name().
5 FutureWarning: 'M' is deprecated, please use 'ME' Using deprecated frequency alias letters from older Pandas 1.x versions. Adopt modern Pandas 2.x standard: 'ME' (Month End), 'MS' (Month Start), 'h' (Hourly), 'min' (Minutes).
6 FutureWarning: The default fill_method='pad' in pct_change is deprecated Calling pct_change() without specifying fill behavior. Always write df['val'].pct_change(fill_method=None).
7 TypeError: Only valid with DatetimeIndex, TimedeltaIndex or PeriodIndex Calling df.resample('D') when DataFrame index is a regular RangeIndex. Set the index first via df = df.set_index('timestamp') or pass on='timestamp'.
8 TypeError: Cannot compare tz-naive and tz-aware timestamps Attempting mathematical comparison between localized UTC timestamp and plain local string. Standardize all timestamps: s.dt.tz_localize('UTC') before filtering or slicing.
9 Month length arithmetic bug (30-day assumption) Adding pd.Timedelta(days=30) to January 31 lands on March 2 instead of February end. Use calendar-aware pd.DateOffset(months=1) which correctly snaps to February 28.
10 Data leakage in ML time series train-test split Using random row shuffle (train_test_split(shuffle=True)) leaking future into past. Always perform chronological time splits: past 80% for train, latest 20% for test.
11 Overwriting original timezone during conversion Calling tz_localize() on an already timezone-aware Series raises TypeError. Use tz_convert() if timezone already exists; use tz_localize(None) to strip timezone.
12 Dropping valid intra-interval data with asfreq() Using asfreq() when expecting cumulative sales volume across an interval. Use resample().sum() for volume totals; keep asfreq() only for instantaneous gauge snapshots.
13 Forward-filling before resampling creating phantom data Running ffill() on raw irregularly spaced rows before aggregating into bins. Always aggregate into regular bins first via resample(), then apply imputation methods.
14 String subtraction runtime failure Executing df['check_out'] - df['check_in'] when columns are stored as object/string. Convert columns with pd.to_datetime() first before calculating differences.

Part 1: 5 Beginner Conceptual & Syntax Questions

Q1: What is the fundamental difference between pd.Timestamp and Python's built-in datetime.datetime?

Answer: Python ka standard datetime.datetime microsecond (\(10^{-6}\) seconds) precision support karta hai aur pure Python object ke taur par store hota hai jisse large datasets par loop karna slow ho jata hai.

Pandas ka pd.Timestamp internally NumPy ke datetime64[ns] format par built hai, jo nanosecond (\(10^{-9}\) seconds) precision support karta hai, C-contiguous contiguous memory blocks me vectorize hota hai, aur rich accessors (.dt) aur calendar offsets provide karta hai.

Q2: Why does pd.to_datetime('15/10/2026') fail or misbehave without dayfirst=True?

Answer: By default, pd.to_datetime() American standard MM/DD/YYYY format follow karta hai. String '15/10/2026' me pehla number 15 hai, aur calendar me 15th month exist nahi karta! Agar '05/10/2026' ho to silently 10th May parse kar lega jabki aapka intended date 5th October tha.

Indian aur European formats ke liye hamesha dayfirst=True pass karein ya strict format='%d/%m/%Y' use karein.

Q3: Why can't we write df['timestamp'].year directly on a Series?

Answer: Ek pd.Timestamp scalar object ke paas direct .year attribute hota hai. Lekin jab timestamps ek pd.Series ke andar hote hain, tab series ke columns aur generic methods ke sath namespace collision prevent karne ke liye Pandas saare datetime properties ko .dt namespace accessor ke andar encapsulate karta hai (e.g. df['timestamp'].dt.year).

Q4: What is NaT in Pandas and how is it related to np.nan?

Answer: NaT stands for "Not a Time". Yeh Pandas ka specialized sentinel value hai jo datetime aur timedelta columns me missing values ko represent karta hai (equivalent to np.nan for float columns). pd.isna(pd.NaT) returns True.

Q5: What is the modern replacement for the deprecated 'M' frequency alias in Pandas 2.x?

Answer: Pandas 2.x me 'M' deprecate ho chuka hai kyunki yeh ambiguous tha (Month End ya Minute). Modern syntax me Month End ke liye 'ME', Month Start ke liye 'MS', Hourly ke liye lowercase 'h', aur Minute ke liye 'min' use kiya jata hai.

Q8: What is the difference between adding pd.Timedelta(days=30) and pd.DateOffset(months=1) to January 31?

  • Timedelta(days=30) ek absolute physical clock offset hai (exactly \(30 imes 24 = 720\) hours). January 31 me 30 days add karne par result March 2 aayega.
  • DateOffset(months=1) calendar-aware semantic increment karta hai. January 31 me 1 month offset add karne par Pandas automatically February ke end (February 28) par snap karta hai.

Q10: Why does s.pct_change() trigger a Future warning in modern Pandas, and what is the fix?

21. Master Summary, High-Density Cheat Sheet & What's Next

Aapne Pandas ke sabse powerful aur industry-critical module DateTime & Time Series Data ko master kar liya hai! Niche diye gaye summary bullet points aur high-density cheat sheet table ko revise karein.

Core Conceptual Takeaways

  • String vs DateTime: Dates ko string ke bajaye hamesha datetime64[ns] format me convert karein via pd.to_datetime() taaki vectorized arithmetic aur date filtering enable ho sake.
  • The .dt Accessor: Series ke har timestamp se temporal components (year, month, hour, day_name) extract karne ke liye .dt namespace mandatory hai.
  • DatetimeIndex: Index me datetime hone par partial-string slicing (df.loc['2026-10']) aur chronological resampling automate ho jati hai.
  • Timedelta vs DateOffset: Physical exact time differences (seconds, hours, days) ke liye pd.Timedelta use karein; calendar month/year snapping ke liye pd.DateOffset use karein.
  • Resampling vs Rolling: resample() frequency change karke rows aggregate karta hai; rolling() fixed window move karke noise smooth karta hai without changing row count.
  • shift(), diff(), and pct_change(): Period-over-period growth aur trends track karne ke universal building blocks hain. Modern Pandas me hamesha pct_change(fill_method=None) likhein.
  • Time Zones: Multi-region systems me naive timestamps ko UTC me localize karein (tz_localize('UTC')), fir regional timezone me convert karein (tz_convert('Asia/Kolkata')).
